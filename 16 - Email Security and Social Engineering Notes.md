# Email Security and Social Engineering Notes

## Overview

These notes summarise key concepts related to email security, phishing attacks, social engineering techniques, email spoofing, malware delivery, authentication mechanisms, and defensive cybersecurity practices.

Main areas covered:
- Email security fundamentals
- Social engineering attacks
- Phishing techniques
- Email spoofing
- Malware distribution
- Credential theft
- Email authentication technologies
- Defensive security strategies

---

# Email Security Fundamentals

## Purpose of Email Security

Email security protects:
- Email communication
- User credentials
- Sensitive information
- Organisational systems

from:
- Phishing attacks
- Malware
- Spoofing
- Spam
- Unauthorised access

---

# Email Communication Risks

## Common Threats

Email systems are frequently targeted because they are widely used for:
- Authentication
- Business communication
- File sharing
- Financial transactions

Attackers exploit human behaviour and trust relationships.

---

# Social Engineering

## Definition

Social engineering manipulates individuals into revealing sensitive information or performing insecure actions.

Attackers exploit:
- Trust
- Fear
- Urgency
- Curiosity
- Authority

---

# Phishing Attacks

## Definition

Phishing attacks attempt to deceive users into disclosing:
- Passwords
- Financial information
- Authentication credentials
- Personal information

Attackers often impersonate trusted organisations.

---

# Common Phishing Techniques

## Email Phishing

Fraudulent emails designed to appear legitimate.

Characteristics:
- Fake login pages
- Suspicious links
- Urgent language
- Malicious attachments

---

## Spear Phishing

Highly targeted phishing attacks directed at specific individuals or organisations.

Attackers collect:
- Employee information
- Organisational structure
- Social media data
- Public records

Targeted attacks appear more convincing.

---

## Whaling

Phishing attacks targeting high-profile individuals such as:
- Executives
- Managers
- Administrators

Whaling attacks often aim for:
- Financial fraud
- Confidential information
- Administrative access

---

# Email Spoofing

## Concept

Email spoofing forges sender information to impersonate trusted sources.

Spoofed emails may appear to originate from:
- Banks
- Government agencies
- Universities
- Technology companies
- Internal departments

---

# Malicious Links

## Purpose

Phishing emails commonly contain links that redirect victims to:
- Fake websites
- Credential harvesting pages
- Malware downloads

Attackers imitate legitimate websites to increase credibility.

---

# Credential Theft

## Attack Objective

Credential theft attacks aim to capture:
- Usernames
- Passwords
- Authentication tokens
- Banking information

Compromised credentials may lead to:
- Account takeover
- Data breaches
- Financial loss

---

# Malicious Attachments

## Risks

Phishing emails may contain infected attachments such as:
- PDF files
- Office documents
- ZIP archives
- Executable files

Attachments may deliver:
- Malware
- Ransomware
- Keyloggers
- Remote access trojans

---

# Malware Distribution

## Email-Based Malware Delivery

Email remains one of the most common malware distribution channels.

Malicious payloads may:
- Encrypt files
- Steal information
- Install spyware
- Establish remote control access

---

# Website Impersonation

## Fake Websites

Attackers create websites that visually imitate legitimate services.

Common techniques:
- Similar domain names
- Copied branding
- Replicated login pages
- Fake security warnings

---

# URL Manipulation

## Deceptive URLs

Attackers manipulate URLs using:
- Misspelled domains
- Similar characters
- Hidden redirects
- Fake subdomains

Example:
- `paypa1.com` instead of `paypal.com`

---

# Psychological Manipulation

## Common Tactics

Social engineering attacks commonly use:
- Urgency
- Fear
- Curiosity
- Authority
- Rewards

Examples:
- “Immediate action required”
- “Account suspension notice”
- “Security alert detected”

---

# Indicators of Suspicious Emails

## Warning Signs

Potential phishing indicators include:
- Spelling errors
- Unusual sender addresses
- Unexpected attachments
- Suspicious links
- Generic greetings
- Requests for credentials

---

# Email Authentication Technologies

## SPF (Sender Policy Framework)

SPF verifies authorised email sending servers.

Purpose:
- Reduces email spoofing

---

## DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail)

DKIM uses digital signatures to verify email integrity.

Purpose:
- Detects message tampering

---

## DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance)

DMARC improves email authentication policies.

Purpose:
- Reduces phishing and spoofing attacks

---

# Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

## Purpose

MFA adds additional verification beyond passwords.

Examples:
- SMS verification
- Authentication apps
- Hardware tokens
- Biometrics

MFA reduces risks from stolen credentials.

---

# Security Awareness Training

## Importance

User awareness is one of the strongest phishing defences.

Training helps users:
- Identify phishing attempts
- Verify suspicious emails
- Avoid malicious links
- Report security incidents

---

# Incident Response

## Recommended Actions

If phishing is suspected:
1. Avoid clicking suspicious links
2. Disconnect compromised systems if necessary
3. Change passwords immediately
4. Report the incident
5. Scan systems for malware
6. Enable MFA protections

---

# Defensive Security Practices

## Recommended Protections

- Use multi-factor authentication
- Implement email filtering systems
- Verify suspicious URLs
- Apply security awareness training
- Keep systems updated
- Use endpoint protection software
- Monitor email activity
- Restrict attachment execution

---

# Security Risks

## Potential Consequences

Email security failures may result in:
- Credential theft

- Financial fraud
- Identity theft
- Data breaches
- Unauthorised access
