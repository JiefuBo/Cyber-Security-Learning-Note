# Cybersecurity Management And Threat Intelligence

## Introduction
Cybersecurity focuses on protecting information systems, networks, applications, and digital assets against unauthorized access, attacks, disruption, and misuse. Cybercrime refers to illegal activities conducted using computers, networks, or digital technologies. Modern cybersecurity combines technical controls, policies, risk management, governance, and operational security practices.

---

## Core Cybersecurity Concepts

### Cybersecurity
- Protection of confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information systems.
- Prevention of unauthorized access, malware infections, data theft, and service disruption.
- Integration of people, processes, and technologies to reduce security risks.

### Cybercrime
- Illegal activities performed through digital systems or networks.
- Includes phishing, ransomware, identity theft, data breaches, malware distribution, and financial fraud.
- Cybercriminal motivations include financial gain, espionage, political influence, and disruption.

### Recent Cybersecurity Incidents
- Large-scale attacks demonstrate the impact of ransomware, data leaks, supply chain attacks, and credential theft.
- Common attack vectors include phishing emails, vulnerable software, weak passwords, and insecure cloud services.
- Incident response requires rapid detection, containment, recovery, and post-incident analysis.

---

## Security Goals

### Confidentiality
- Ensures that information is only accessible to authorized individuals.
- Implemented through encryption, authentication, and access control.

### Integrity
- Protects data from unauthorized modification or destruction.
- Maintained using hashing, digital signatures, validation, and auditing.

### Availability
- Ensures systems and services remain accessible when needed.
- Supported by backups, redundancy, disaster recovery, and fault tolerance.

### Additional Security Principles
- Authentication verifies user identity.
- Authorization controls access permissions.
- Accountability tracks user actions through logs and monitoring.
- Non-repudiation prevents denial of performed actions.

## Cybersecurity Management

### Security Governance
- Establishes organizational policies, procedures, and security responsibilities.
- Aligns security strategies with business objectives and compliance requirements.

### Risk Management
- Identifies, analyzes, evaluates, and mitigates security risks.
- Includes asset identification, threat analysis, vulnerability assessment, and impact evaluation.

### Security Policies
- Define acceptable use, access control, password management, and incident handling.
- Promote consistency and compliance within organizations.

### Compliance and Standards
- Security frameworks and standards improve governance and operational consistency.
- Examples include ISO 27001, NIST Cybersecurity Framework, and organizational policies.

## Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI)

### Definition
Cyber Threat Intelligence refers to the collection, analysis, and sharing of information related to cyber threats, threat actors, vulnerabilities, and attack techniques.

### Types of Threat Intelligence

#### Strategic Intelligence
- High-level analysis supporting management and long-term decision-making.
- Focuses on trends, risks, and organizational impact.

#### Tactical Intelligence
- Describes attack methods, techniques, and procedures (TTPs).
- Supports defensive planning and detection improvement.

#### Operational Intelligence
- Provides information about ongoing or imminent attacks.
- Helps incident response and threat hunting activities.

#### Technical Intelligence
- Includes indicators of compromise (IOCs) such as IP addresses, domains, malware hashes, and signatures.

## Key Takeaways
- Cybersecurity combines governance, technical controls, risk management, and operational security.
- Security objectives focus on confidentiality, integrity, and availability.
- Threat intelligence supports proactive detection and defense against evolving threats.
- Risk assessment identifies vulnerabilities and determines mitigation priorities.
- Secure coding practices reduce software vulnerabilities and improve system resilience.
- Continuous monitoring, incident response, and security awareness are essential for maintaining organizational security.
